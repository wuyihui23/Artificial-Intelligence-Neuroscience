Split Attractor Lab

Two simulations of the paper's rate model. The first shows why slow excitation with fast inhibition makes persistent activity robust. The second shows how inhibiting one half of the split h∆K–PFG ring switches the circuit between following the compass and storing a goal.

Rig 1 · Extended Data Fig. 7

Persistence needs slow excitation

One excitatory unit (all h∆K + PFG lumped) excites itself and an inhibitory unit, which inhibits it back. Only the synaptic outputs U (excitation) and V (inhibition) are kept as variables:

U' = a_exc·( F(W_EE·U − W_IE·V + I) − U ) V' = a_inh·( max(U, 0) − V ) F clips to [0, 1]; 1/a = synaptic decay time

When inhibition is fast enough to track excitation (V ≈ U), the loop behaves like one leaky unit with net gain g = W_EE − W_IE. After the input ends, activity decays with

τ ≈ 1 / ( a_exc · (1 − g) ) for g < 1 g ≥ 1 → activity latches at saturation
  • τ scales with 1/a_exc. Slow excitation (a = 0.64, decay 1.6 s) stretches every decay about 7× compared with fast excitation (a = 4.63).
  • So the band of gains that gives seconds-long persistence is about 7× wider. Small weight changes tune τ smoothly instead of flipping the network between "transient" and "stuck on".
  • With fast excitation, inhibition also lags behind and the loop overshoots, which narrows the useful band further.

Disinhibition writes the heading

30 h∆K and 18 PFG units sit around the fan-shaped body ring and excite each other with Gaussian weights (σ = 0.25 rad). One global inhibitory unit, driven by all of them, inhibits both. Only PFG receives the compass bump, at 1/6 of the recurrent strength.

τ·u_h∆K' = −10·u_h∆K + exc·W·U_PFG − U_inh + I_h∆K τ·u_PFG' = −10·u_PFG + exc·W·U_h∆K − U_inh + compass U'' = −(a+b)·U' + a·b·( F(u) − U ) (alpha synapse)
  • FB5V inhibition on (I_h∆K = −15, turning): h∆K is silent, so the loop is open. PFG shows a weak bump that follows the compass.
  • Released (I_h∆K = 0, odour run): the loop closes. Recurrence is about 6× stronger than the compass input, so the bump grows and locks where the heading was at the moment of release.
  • Inhibit h∆K again and the stored bump fades over a few seconds (slow PFG synapses), then PFG follows the compass again.

Try it: let the compass wander, press release, and watch the PFG kymograph stop tracking the heading line.

The gate level (−15), exc = 10 and global inhibition weight 8 were tuned here so the bump stays narrow and the gate fully silences h∆K; the paper used exc ≤ 7 and −5 with its own weight scaling. The model does not include the short-term depression of inhibition seen in the recordings.
